# Civilian life under Russian occupation, Ukraine 2022–2023

Research for 東北：俄羅斯入侵 (long-term survival under occupation). Compiled 2026-11-25. Kept in the repository as the source for every occupation rule (see docs/DESIGN.md, "Long-term survival").

## How to read this file

Each number or rule carries one tag:

- **[R] reported**: the figure or fact is stated in the cited source.
- **[D] derived**: worked out here from reported figures. The arithmetic is shown.
- **[P] play**: the source gives the shape, and the number is a game choice.

Strength of evidence is marked separately:

- **pattern**: several independent sources and places describe the same thing.
- **single**: one testimony or one case.
- **claim**: a statement by a party to the war (a Ukrainian official, a Russian-installed official, an exiled mayor). Not independently verified.

Sources marked † were read only through search-engine summaries, because the page refused automated access (Ukrainska Pravda, IWPR, ISW and a few others) or was not opened. Everything else was opened and read. ISW blocked direct access, so ISW findings appear only as other outlets reported them.

Caveats that apply throughout:

- UN (OHCHR/HRMMU) counts are documented minimums. Russia gave UN monitors no access to occupied territory ([OHCHR, 20 Mar 2024](https://ukraine.un.org/en/node/264055)).
- Ukrainian officials' figures (mayors, prosecutors, military intelligence) and Russian-installed officials' figures are claims.
- Most cold-weather evidence comes from Ukrainian late winter (Kyiv region and Mariupol in March 2022) and from the 2022–23 winter in Mariupol and Luhansk oblast. Heihe in January is much colder. Every cold number for Heihe is therefore [D] or [P], never [R].

## 0. Two kinds of occupation

The sources describe two different situations, and a district in the game should be one or the other.

**A. Assault or siege districts** (Mariupol, Izium, Hostomel, Bucha, Irpin). Fighting cuts utilities within about a day. People live in basements. Soldiers are combat troops who search, loot and kill on the spot. **pattern**

- Mariupol: power, running water and almost all mobile signal lost around 2 March 2022 [R] ([HRW, 21 Mar 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/03/21/ukraine-ensure-safe-passage-aid-mariupol-civilians)).
- Izium: no gas, electricity, running water or phone for the six months of occupation [R] ([AP, 26 Sep 2022](https://www.click2houston.com/news/world/2022/09/26/hardship-remains-for-ukrainian-town-emerging-from-occupation/)).
- Bucha: residents had limited access to water, food, electricity, heating and mobile service [R] ([HRW, 21 Apr 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/04/21/ukraine-russian-forces-trail-death-bucha)).

**B. Administered districts** (Kherson, Melitopol, Berdiansk). The city is taken with little street fighting. Utilities mostly keep running. The danger shifts over months from soldiers to security services, paperwork and informers. **pattern**

- Kherson, four weeks in: transport working, city services on shortened hours [R] ([Kyiv Independent, 28 Mar 2022](https://kyivindependent.com/national/facing-resistance-in-occupied-kherson-russian-forces-crack-down-on-disobedient-residents)).
- Kherson, mid-September: the occupation administration said power and water were working, with outages recorded since 25 August [R, claim] ([CIR, Sep 2022](https://info-res.org/app/uploads/2024/11/20220920_Kherson-series-1_Mechanisms-of-occupation-FINAL.pdf)).
- The same CIR report sums it up: soldiers invade, but bureaucrats, bankers and security services do the occupying.

## 1. The first hours and days

### 1.1 What soldiers did in streets and homes

- **Bucha.** Soon after taking the town, troops went door to door through apartment buildings, saying they were hunting "Nazis." They searched for weapons, questioned residents and sometimes took men away. Families were not told where [R, pattern] ([HRW, 21 Apr 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/04/21/ukraine-russian-forces-trail-death-bucha)).
  - In one building, troops ordered everyone into the basement and told them to leave their doors unlocked. They then forced a locked door and wrecked the flat [R, single].
- **Hostomel.** Troops stormed an apartment block on 5 March. They ordered residents out with hands up and searched them for weapons and communication devices [R, single] ([Al Jazeera, 5 Jul 2022](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2022/7/5/like-ghosts-inside-an-occupied-apartment-building-in-ukraine)).
  - Phones were forbidden. Two residents hid spare ones.
  - Soldiers slept in residents' beds and damaged flats. A soldier warned one man that the next rotation would kill him.
- **Kherson, first month.**
  - Armed men visited homes of known protesters and activists, sometimes at 6 a.m., and warned of "serious problems" [R] ([Kyiv Independent, 28 Mar 2022](https://kyivindependent.com/national/facing-resistance-in-occupied-kherson-russian-forces-crack-down-on-disobedient-residents)).
  - Daily pro-Ukrainian protests began in early March. They were first tolerated, then broken up with Rosgvardia units, tear gas, stun grenades and live fire. They shrank from thousands of people to under a hundred, and the last one was in May [R, pattern] ([Kyiv Independent, 28 Mar 2022](https://kyivindependent.com/national/facing-resistance-in-occupied-kherson-russian-forces-crack-down-on-disobedient-residents); [Kyiv Independent, 28 Jul 2022](https://kyivindependent.com/national/amid-brutal-russian-occupation-khersons-resistance-continues-through-boycott-guerilla-operations)).
- **Berdiansk.** Local journalists were detained and their phones, documents and computers examined. Protesters were beaten and interrogated [R] ([RFE/RL, 12 Apr 2022](https://www.rferl.org/a/ukraine-berdyansk-russia-occupation-life/31798991.html)).
- **Kyiv and Chernihiv villages.** In Nova Basan, soldiers detained every man on one street as they took the village. In Yahidne, they herded almost the whole village into the school basement "for their safety" [R] ([HRW, 18 May 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/05/18/ukraine-executions-torture-during-russian-occupation)).

### 1.2 Door-to-door searches: what they looked for

Items and traits that led to detention in the sources [R, pattern]:

- Weapons and communication devices ([HRW Bucha](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/04/21/ukraine-russian-forces-trail-death-bucha); [Al Jazeera Hostomel](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2022/7/5/like-ghosts-inside-an-occupied-apartment-building-in-ukraine)).
- Phone content:
  - pro-Ukrainian Telegram channels found on a 16-year-old's phone;
  - photos of Russian military equipment;
  - messages about Russian vehicles;
  - a phone showing contact with Ukrainian forces ([HRW, 22 Jul 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/07/22/ukraine-torture-disappearances-occupied-south); [HRW, 18 May 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/05/18/ukraine-executions-torture-during-russian-occupation); [Kyiv Independent, 22 Apr 2023](https://kyivindependent.com/theyll-kill-me-if-i-come-back-abduction-torture-become-routine-in-russian-occupied-melitopol/)).
- Camouflage clothing, a text message, or a record of military service. The head of the UN monitoring mission said any of these could prove fatal [R] ([OHCHR press release, Dec 2022](https://ukraine.un.org/en/node/210464)).
- Clothing, drones, Ukrainian symbols and "patriotic" tattoos ([OHCHR detention report, 27 Jun 2023, paras 40–53](https://ukraine.un.org/en/238279-detention-civilians-context-armed-attack-russian-federation-against-ukraine)).
- Owning a Ukrainian flag, or cursing Putin. In Balakliia, arrests happened at checkpoints, markets, homes and parks ([NHC fact sheet](https://nhc.no/content/uploads/2025/08/Fact-sheet-Balakliia-version-2.pdf)).
- In Melitopol by 2023: speaking Ukrainian, holding hryvnias, having Ukraine's "Dia" state app, refusing conscription papers, or having travelled to Ukrainian-held territory [R, single source] ([Kyiv Independent, 22 Apr 2023](https://kyivindependent.com/theyll-kill-me-if-i-come-back-abduction-torture-become-routine-in-russian-occupied-melitopol/)).

**Where people were taken.** In Kherson oblast, over half of the 226 documented abductions happened at the victim's home [R] ([Yale HRL, 18 Nov 2022](https://medicine.yale.edu/news-article/extrajudicial-detentions-and-enforced-disappearances-in-kherson-oblast)).

**How often.** No source gives a count of searches per flat or per block. The shape the sources give [R, pattern]:

- an early sweep of every building in the first days (Bucha, Hostomel);
- later, targeted visits.

Kherson residents warned each other over messenger apps when a search party entered their area. In other words, searches came as waves through a neighbourhood, not as random single visits ([hromadske, 27 Nov 2022](https://hromadske.ua/en/posts/eight-months-of-fear-and-resistance-stories-heard-and-seen-in-kherson)).

### 1.3 Phones, tattoos, social media

- **At filtration points (Mariupol area):**
  - fingerprints, palm prints, and front and side photos;
  - phones searched for contacts, messages and photos. One woman's phone was plugged into a computer for about 20 minutes; another's contacts were copied by Bluetooth;
  - men examined for military or nationalist tattoos and for bruises suggesting they had carried a rifle [R, pattern] ([HRW, 1 Sep 2022](https://www.hrw.org/report/2022/09/01/we-had-no-choice/filtration-and-crime-forcibly-transferring-ukrainian-civilians)).
- **At checkpoints on the way out of Berdiansk:** men were made to undress for tattoo checks, and their fingers were inspected for gun lubricant. Phones were searched, and a man was beaten over a meme on his phone [R, single article] ([RFE/RL, 12 Apr 2022](https://www.rferl.org/a/ukraine-berdyansk-russia-occupation-life/31798991.html)).
- **At Kherson city checkpoints:** phones were checked, messengers and social media included. Guards found it suspicious when people had no account on the Russian social network VKontakte. Having nothing was also a flag [R, single] ([hromadske, 27 Nov 2022](https://hromadske.ua/en/posts/eight-months-of-fear-and-resistance-stories-heard-and-seen-in-kherson)).
- **In Kherson after September 2022:** a detention officer used a device to match a man's face and his phone photos against protest images going back to 2014 [R, single] ([HRW, 13 Apr 2023](https://www.hrw.org/news/2023/04/13/ukraine-russian-torture-center-kherson)).
- **In Andriivka (Kyiv oblast):** soldiers used a device that detected mobile phone use [R, single] ([HRW, 18 May 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/05/18/ukraine-executions-torture-during-russian-occupation)).
- **How people adapted:** families in Melitopol avoided Russian SIM cards because they assumed monitoring, and did not carry phones outside [R, single] ([Kyiv Independent, 22 Apr 2023](https://kyivindependent.com/theyll-kill-me-if-i-come-back-abduction-torture-become-routine-in-russian-occupied-melitopol/)). In occupied Mariupol, using a VPN could lead to police questioning [R, single] ([Meduza, 6 Dec 2022](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2022/12/06/we-ve-been-left-to-die)).

### 1.4 Curfews and movement rules

**Kherson, day 2 (2 March 2022).** The mayor relayed conditions set by the "armed visitors" [R]:

- curfew from 20:00 to 06:00;
- vehicles may enter the city only by day, and only to bring food and medicine;
- people may walk only alone or in pairs;
- residents must stop at the first demand;
- cars must drive at minimum speed and accept searches;
- residents must not provoke the military ([Ukrainska Pravda, 2 Mar 2022](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2022/03/02/7327540/)).

**Bucha.** Residents were generally barred from leaving their buildings except to fetch water, and sometimes even that was forbidden [R] ([HRW, 21 Apr 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/04/21/ukraine-russian-forces-trail-death-bucha)). Troops made residents wear white armbands to mark themselves as civilians, according to eight residents [R] ([Reuters via RNZ, 4 Apr 2022](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/world/464613/in-a-ukrainian-street-a-corpse-with-hands-bound-and-a-bullet-wound-to-the-head)†).

**Berdiansk.** Soldiers enforced a curfew in the city centre. The article gives no hours [R] ([RFE/RL, 12 Apr 2022](https://www.rferl.org/a/ukraine-berdyansk-russia-occupation-life/31798991.html)).

**Kherson, last week (4 November 2022).** A Russian-installed official announced a round-the-clock curfew, then withdrew it within about an hour [R] ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/nobot/russian-proxy-reverses-statement-on-curfew-in-kherson)†).

### 1.5 Shooting of civilians in streets and cars

**Overall (Kyiv, Chernihiv and Sumy regions).** The UN documented 441 civilians killed while those areas were under Russian control, 24 February to 6 April 2022 [R] ([OHCHR, Dec 2022](https://ukraine.un.org/en/node/210464)).

- Victims: 341 men, 72 women, 20 boys and 8 girls (77% men).
- Of 100 killings examined in detail, 57 were summary executions. Thirty of these happened in places of detention, and 27 victims were killed on the spot after troops took control.
- Men and boys were 88% of summary-execution victims.
- The UN said true numbers are likely considerably higher.

**People killed while moving.** In 43 further cases, people were hit while moving on foot, by bicycle, car or van. They were mostly going to work, bringing food to others, visiting relatives or fleeing [R] ([OHCHR thematic report, 9 Dec 2022, executive summary](https://ukraine.un.org/en/node/210727)†).

**Bucha** [R] ([HRW, 21 Apr 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/04/21/ukraine-russian-forces-trail-death-bucha)):

- Residents described troops shooting indiscriminately at civilians who went outside.
- Cases: a man shot on his balcony while smoking (7 Mar); a man shot while fetching food from his garage (20 Mar).
- A local official counted 278 bodies, mostly civilians, by 15 April. The town had about 36,000 people before the war.
- The UN had documented 73 civilian killings in Bucha by October 2022, with 105 more being checked ([OHCHR](https://ukraine.un.org/en/node/210464)).
- A New York Times visual investigation tied the Yablunska Street killings to the 234th Air Assault Regiment ([summary of the NYT investigation, Fakty, 22 Dec 2022](https://fakty.com.ua/en/proisshestvija/20221222-zhurnalisty-nyt-identyfikuvaly-desyatky-soldativ-armiyi-rf-prychetnyh-do-vbyvstv-ukrayincziv-u-buchi)†).

**Irpin.** On 5–6 March, troops fired on ten civilian cars trying to leave, killing nine people [R, claim: Ukrainian police] ([Babel](https://babel.ua/en/news/91268-ukraine-identified-russian-soldiers-who-shot-10-cars-of-civilians-leaving-irpin-last-march-then-9-people-died)†). Shelling also hit an evacuation crossing by the destroyed bridge on 6 March [R] ([HRW, 8 Mar 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/03/08/ukraine-russian-assault-kills-fleeing-civilians)†).

**Hostomel.** The mayor was shot while delivering food and medicine to residents in early March. Sources give 3 or 7 March [R] ([NV](https://english.nv.ua/nation/russian-invaders-kill-the-mayor-of-hostomel-50222919.html)†; [Egypt Independent, reprinting a CNN account](https://www.egyptindependent.com/gunned-down-on-a-mercy-mission-witnesses-say-russian-troops-killed-hostomel-mayor-and-two-volunteers/)†).

**Kherson.** A resident said Russian military trucks deliberately rammed civilian cars in about ten cases [R, single] ([hromadske, 27 Nov 2022](https://hromadske.ua/en/posts/eight-months-of-fear-and-resistance-stories-heard-and-seen-in-kherson)).

### 1.6 Looting

- **Bucha:** TVs, appliances, jewellery, food and household goods. Belongings were strapped on vehicles. One safe was cut open with a grinder [R, pattern] ([HRW, 21 Apr 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/04/21/ukraine-russian-forces-trail-death-bucha)).
- **Hostomel:** TVs, vacuum cleaners, shoes and clothing [R, single] ([Al Jazeera, 5 Jul 2022](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2022/7/5/like-ghosts-inside-an-occupied-apartment-building-in-ukraine)).
- **Dymer:** about US$11,000 cash from a passport folder. After release, people's houses were looted of a car, a generator, tools and cash [R] ([HRW, 18 May 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/05/18/ukraine-executions-torture-during-russian-occupation)).
- **Kherson:** troops reportedly looted supermarkets first, then smaller shops [R, claim] ([Kyiv Independent, 28 Mar 2022](https://kyivindependent.com/national/facing-resistance-in-occupied-kherson-russian-forces-crack-down-on-disobedient-residents)). A car repair shop owner was told to pay 5,000 hryvnia or repair military cars for free. A pastor's car, about $2,000 of medicine and aid, and about $6,000 cash were taken [R] ([HRW, 22 Jul 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/07/22/ukraine-torture-disappearances-occupied-south)).
- **Balakliia:** soldiers ransacked every store in town, emptied homes and killed farm animals [R] ([Meduza, 16 Sep 2022](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2022/09/16/anyone-could-be-taken-captive-at-any-time)).
- **Shipping loot home.** Mediazona tracked shipments from courier offices near the border, February to May 2022 [R] ([Mediazona, 27 May 2022](https://en.zona.media/article/2022/05/27/marauders_eng)):
  - about 13.5 t from Valuiki, over 13 t from Klintsy, about 4.5 t from Mazyr in Belarus;
  - daily weight peaked near 4 t on 4 April, as troops left the Kyiv region;
  - items included TVs, tyres, tools, food and a military drone.

## 2. The first weeks

### 2.1 Checkpoints and filtration: what passing one involved, and how long

**Inside a city**

- Kherson had three Russian checkpoints. At the worst one, on the island in Korabelnyi district, people often queued for several hours [R, single] ([hromadske, 27 Nov 2022](https://hromadske.ua/en/posts/eight-months-of-fear-and-resistance-stories-heard-and-seen-in-kherson)).

**Leaving a besieged city (Mariupol, mid-March 2022)** [R] ([HRW, 21 Mar 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/03/21/ukraine-ensure-safe-passage-aid-mariupol-civilians)):

- Drivers passed 15 to 20 Russian checkpoints, where soldiers checked documents, phones and hands. One man was detained.
- Private convoys took 24 to 72 hours.
- About 160 cars left on 14 March and several thousand people on 15–16 March. At least 3,200 people reached Zaporizhzhia on 16 March.

**Leaving the occupied south (Vasylivka crossing, summer 2022)**

From [ZMINA, 8 Sep 2022](https://zmina.info/?p=215283) [R]:

- One family counted 30 checkpoints on the road to Vasylivka, with documents checked at each.
- Families waited four to five nights near Vasylivka.
- A volunteer estimated that 100 to 200 cars were let through a day. The Melitopol mayor said 20 to 150 [claim].
- Inspecting ten cars took 30 to 60 minutes. That is about 3 to 6 minutes per car [D].
- Laptops and tablets were checked in a separate building. Buses were fully unloaded.
- One man had to strip to show tattoos at two of the 30 checkpoints, and had his underwear searched at another [single].
- Men were asked about military service and jobs. Some were taken off buses.
- Private carriers charged 3,000 to 7,000 hryvnia. CIR saw social-media adverts from about 7,000 hryvnia (about £165) per person ([CIR, Sep 2022](https://info-res.org/app/uploads/2024/11/20220920_Kherson-series-1_Mechanisms-of-occupation-FINAL.pdf)).
- Hazards: shelling in the grey zone, about 40 minutes of road. More than ten people had died in the queue, and people reported strokes, heart attacks and dysentery.
- Kakhovka to Odesa took about four hours before the war and six days at the time of the article.

Other reports:

- In October 2022, about 6,000 cars were waiting at Vasylivka, people were living in cars for over ten days, and five people died in the queue in one week [R, claim: Melitopol mayor] ([hromadske](https://hromadske.ua/en/posts/russian-forces-hold-more-than-5000-people-in-the-queue-at-the-checkpoint-in-vasylivka-5-people-have-died-mayor)†; [Ukrainska Pravda, 8 Oct 2022](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2022/10/08/7370945/)†).
  - Caution [D]: 6,000 cars at 20–200 cars a day would mean a 30- to 300-day wait, far longer than the 4 to 10 days people reported. Queue sizes and throughput varied, so do not combine the two figures.
- One protester needed three days to cover 200 km from Kherson to Kryvyi Rih [R, single] ([HRW, 22 Jul 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/07/22/ukraine-torture-disappearances-occupied-south)).

**Filtration (Mariupol area, March–May 2022)** [R, pattern] ([HRW, 1 Sep 2022](https://www.hrw.org/report/2022/09/01/we-had-no-choice/filtration-and-crime-forcibly-transferring-ukrainian-civilians)):

- HRW found 15 sites.
- Waits ran from several hours to almost a month.
  - One man was 666th in line in Manhush on 22 March and waited almost a month. That implies about 22 to 24 people processed a day at that point [D, single].
  - A woman queued about ten days.
- Afterwards most people got a stamped paper receipt, which some had to show at later checkpoints.
- People who "failed" were detained. Men at Bezimenne (allegedly over 600) and Kozatske (about 188) were confined for over 40 days after passing, with their passports withheld.
- Yale HRL mapped at least 21 filtration locations in and around Donetsk oblast ([Yale HRL, 25 Aug 2022](https://medicine.yale.edu/news-article/yale-researchers-identify-21-sites-in-donetsk-oblast-ukraine-used-for-civilian-interrogation-processing-and-detention)†).
- Amnesty interviewed 88 civilians. Forces sometimes refused to let people go west and walked them onto buses heading into Russian-held territory ([Amnesty, 10 Nov 2022](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2022/11/ukraine-russias-unlawful-transfer-of-civilians-a-war-crime-and-likely-a-crime-against-humanity-new-report/)†).

**The receipt becomes an internal pass.** In occupied Mariupol by December 2022, everyone had to pass filtration: phone check, fingerprints and photo. The small paper slip with your name was needed to work and to move around the city [R, single] ([Mediazona, 14 Dec 2022](https://zona.media/article/2022/12/14/mariupolis)).

### 2.2 Lists of targets

Who was targeted [R, pattern]:

- Territorial Defence members, mayors and officials, police, protest participants, journalists, and people thought to hold security information. Later, detentions looked more random, and aid volunteers were targeted too ([HRW, 22 Jul 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/07/22/ukraine-torture-disappearances-occupied-south)).
  - One detainee was told he was in "their database" and on a blacklist [single].
- The UN's 864 Russian-side detention cases include [R] ([OHCHR, 27 Jun 2023, paras 6, 40, 47, 52](https://ukraine.un.org/en/238279-detention-civilians-context-armed-attack-russian-federation-against-ukraine)):
  - 260 people held for perceived views: local officials, activists, aid volunteers, teachers and priests;
  - 88 former soldiers, mostly veterans of the 2014–2022 Donbas operations;
  - at least 10 people held because a relative served in Ukraine's army.
- The UN describes the shift: targeting began with perceived security threats and widened to anyone seen as opposing the occupation [R] ([OHCHR, 20 Mar 2024](https://ukraine.un.org/en/node/264055)).
- How lists were built: as troops advanced, FSB officers seized local government records and used them to compile lists of state employees and home addresses [R, via press coverage of RUSI] ([RUSI, 29 Mar 2023](https://rusi.org/explore-our-research/publications/special-resources/preliminary-lessons-russias-unconventional-operations-during-russo-ukrainian-war-february-2022)†).
- Yale HRL found the profiles of the 226 Kherson victims consistent with a premeditated campaign. Only 9 of them were accused of, or claimed, armed resistance [R] ([Yale HRL, 18 Nov 2022](https://medicine.yale.edu/news-article/extrajudicial-detentions-and-enforced-disappearances-in-kherson-oblast)).
- Rescuers were targeted too. A Kherson region kindergarten head was blacklisted for feeding villagers and helping them evacuate [R, single] ([BBC via RNZ, 24 Oct 2022](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/world/477279/ukraine-war-the-cost-of-occupation-in-kherson-region)).

### 2.3 Detentions, torture sites, and how long people were held

**UN figures, 24 Feb 2022 – 23 May 2023** [R] ([OHCHR detention report, 27 Jun 2023](https://ukraine.un.org/en/238279-detention-civilians-context-armed-attack-russian-federation-against-ukraine); figures from the published text, paras 5–9, 37, 59–60, 66, 94):

- 864 cases of arbitrary detention by Russia: 763 men, 94 women and 7 boys, so 88% men [D].
- Held from several days to weeks or months. Some filtration detainees were held over three months.
- 161 places of detention: 124 in occupied Ukraine, 35 in Russia and 2 in Belarus. 72 of them were makeshift sites.
- 26% of detainees were moved within occupied territory or deported to Russia.
- Over 91% of these detainees described torture or ill-treatment.
- 77 were summarily executed in detention and one more died.
- 354 were released or left behind when forces withdrew.
- Families were usually told nothing for long periods.

**Kherson oblast**

- Yale HRL, March–October 2022 [R] ([Yale HRL, 18 Nov 2022](https://medicine.yale.edu/news-article/extrajudicial-detentions-and-enforced-disappearances-in-kherson-oblast)):
  - 226 cases; fewer than half known to be released;
  - about a quarter allegedly tortured;
  - 5 died in or shortly after custody, about 2.2% [D].
- After liberation, a prosecutor estimated 4,000 to 5,000 registered civilian detention cases, and HRW cited 20 or more detention centres in and around Kherson [R, claim] ([HRW, 13 Apr 2023](https://www.hrw.org/news/2023/04/13/ukraine-russian-torture-center-kherson)).
- Police found 11 places used as prisons, four of them also used for torture [R, claim] ([KHPG, 25 Nov 2022](https://khpg.org/en/1608811434)).

**Conditions**

- Cells held 8 people where 4 were meant, and 11 people in a 2 × 3 m storage room.
- Food was one portion of cereal a day, or three 250 g ration cans per five people every two days. No medical care.
- Detainees were blindfolded and handcuffed throughout ([HRW, 22 Jul 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/07/22/ukraine-torture-disappearances-occupied-south); [HRW, 13 Apr 2023](https://www.hrw.org/news/2023/04/13/ukraine-russian-torture-center-kherson)).
- Melitopol sites included a 2.5 × 3 m cell, cages in a police basement, and metal containers about 2 × 2 m in yards ([Kyiv Independent, 22 Apr 2023](https://kyivindependent.com/theyll-kill-me-if-i-come-back-abduction-torture-become-routine-in-russian-occupied-melitopol/)).

**How people got out** [R] ([HRW, 22 Jul 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/07/22/ukraine-torture-disappearances-occupied-south); [HRW, 13 Apr 2023](https://www.hrw.org/news/2023/04/13/ukraine-russian-torture-center-kherson)):

- Some were released after signing a promise to "cooperate" or filming a video urging others to cooperate.
- One man was required to report Ukrainian checkpoint positions.
- Some were forbidden to leave town.
- One family was asked for a large ransom.

**Other towns**

- Balakliia: at least 282 people detained and 275 tortured or ill-treated, mostly at the police station, held for days to months [R] ([NHC](https://nhc.no/content/uploads/2025/08/Fact-sheet-Balakliia-version-2.pdf)).
- Izium: HRW found seven facilities used to hold and torture residents, and concluded the torture was routine and part of a policy [R] ([HRW, 19 Oct 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/10/19/ukraine-russian-forces-tortured-izium-detainees)).

### 2.4 Food, water, medicine and cash

**Kherson, first month (late March 2022)** [R, mostly resident testimony] ([Kyiv Independent, 28 Mar 2022](https://kyivindependent.com/national/facing-resistance-in-occupied-kherson-russian-forces-crack-down-on-disobedient-residents)):

- Prices up three to five times.
- Flour, butter and sugar scarce.
- Shops closed around 15:00.
- People queued at ATMs from 5 a.m., and withdrawals were capped at 1,000 hryvnia (about $34).
- Insulin and heart and blood-pressure drugs had not been delivered since the occupation began.
- Locals said Russian forces were blocking Ukrainian aid convoys.
- A poultry farm out of feed gave away its 3 million birds.

**Kherson, month 3 (May 2022).** A Ukrainian NGO reported about 90% of pharmacies in the occupied region closed and most drug prices up three- to fivefold [R, claim] ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/ngo-90-of-pharmacies-closed-in-kherson-oblast-drug-prices-rise-3-5-times/amp)†).

**Kherson, month 4–5 (July 2022)**

- Bank cards did not work, and cash could only be bought from private individuals at 10–15% commission. A daily blood thinner rose from about 130 to 900 hryvnia, about 6.9× [D]. Drugs came mostly from Crimea [R, single] ([iStories, 18 Jul 2022](https://istories.media/en/interviews/2022/07/18/stroke-amidst-the-war/)).
- Neither Ukrainian banks nor ATMs worked. People sold belongings at flea markets [R] ([Kyiv Independent, 28 Jul 2022](https://kyivindependent.com/national/amid-brutal-russian-occupation-khersons-resistance-continues-through-boycott-guerilla-operations)).
- Sugar rose from 25 to 80–100 hryvnia per kg, 3.2–4× [D] ([IWPR](https://iwpr.net/gcreash5)†).

**Kherson, month 6–7 (September 2022)** [R] ([CIR, Sep 2022](https://info-res.org/app/uploads/2024/11/20220920_Kherson-series-1_Mechanisms-of-occupation-FINAL.pdf)):

- Packaged food and hygiene goods came mostly from Russia or Crimea, at much higher prices.
- Unlicensed street selling of meat, dairy and medicine spread.
- Pro-occupation channels admitted affordable food was a problem.

**Kherson, end of occupation (November 2022)** [R] ([hromadske, 27 Nov 2022](https://hromadske.ua/en/posts/eight-months-of-fear-and-resistance-stories-heard-and-seen-in-kherson)):

- Food prices about double or more: bananas 150 hryvnia/kg, buckwheat 80–120, meat 250–320.
- Local vegetables did not rise in price, and watermelons cost 5–6 hryvnia/kg.
- Markets often refused rubles.

**Berdiansk (April 2022).** Shops almost empty. Pharmacies had almost nothing in demand. Banks were closed, and some pensions could not be collected [R] ([RFE/RL, 12 Apr 2022](https://www.rferl.org/a/ukraine-berdyansk-russia-occupation-life/31798991.html)).

**Balakliia (six months)** [R] ([Meduza, 16 Sep 2022](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2022/09/16/anyone-could-be-taken-captive-at-any-time)):

- Market food at two to three times pre-war prices. Pensions unpaid. No work.
- People lived off gardens.
- Russian aid was very small: three or four cans of food for three weeks, or half a loaf of bread for a week; one parcel held pasta, condensed milk, mackerel and stewed meat.
- Local farmers sometimes gave away milk.

**Mariupol siege**

- Families ate down their home stocks, then shared. By about three weeks in, one family was on expired pâté and adults ate one meal a day. Baby formula ran out early [R] ([HRW, 21 Mar 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/03/21/ukraine-ensure-safe-passage-aid-mariupol-civilians)).
- Looters broke into shops, and pharmacies were emptied.
- By 12 March, a hospital had run out of oxygen and people with breathing problems were dying.
- MSF staff said there had been no drinking water or medication for over a week, and saw people who died for lack of medication [R] ([MSF, 12 Mar 2022](https://www.doctorswithoutborders.org/latest/ukraine-increasingly-desperate-conditions-mariupol)).
- A survivor said the worst shortage was medication, more than food or heat [R, single] ([HRW, 8 Feb 2024](https://www.hrw.org/news/2024/02/08/ukraine-new-findings-russias-devastation-mariupol)).

### 2.5 How people got water and heat

**Water**

- Mariupol [R, pattern]:
  - melted snow over fires, collected rain, drained building heating systems;
  - queued up to six hours at springs and water trucks while under shelling ([HRW, 21 Mar 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/03/21/ukraine-ensure-safe-passage-aid-mariupol-civilians));
  - dug for ground water and boiled it, at sources one to three km away ([MSF, 12 Mar 2022](https://www.doctorswithoutborders.org/latest/ukraine-increasingly-desperate-conditions-mariupol));
  - in the worst areas, tapped radiators ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/300000-people-trapped-in-besieged-mariupol-face-living-hell)†).
- Mariupol, months later: residents carried 5–6 litre bottles from water trucks [R] ([Meduza, 6 Dec 2022](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2022/12/06/we-ve-been-left-to-die)).
- Izium: rainwater for washing and dishes, plus wells [R] ([AP, 26 Sep 2022](https://www.click2houston.com/news/world/2022/09/26/hardship-remains-for-ukrainian-town-emerging-from-occupation/)). After liberation, an aid group trucked in 80,000 litres [R] ([People in Need](https://www.peopleinneed.net/no-electricity-gas-or-water-supply-people-in-need-and-partners-support-people-in-liberated-areas-in-eastern-ukraine-9508gp)†).
- Kherson at liberation: drinking water from the Vodokanal spring and from the Dnipro [R] ([hromadske, 27 Nov 2022](https://hromadske.ua/en/posts/eight-months-of-fear-and-resistance-stories-heard-and-seen-in-kherson)).

**Heat and cooking**

- Open fires in yards and next to basements in Mariupol [R] ([HRW, 21 Mar 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/03/21/ukraine-ensure-safe-passage-aid-mariupol-civilians)). Generator fuel ran out, and one basement generator was destroyed.
- In Izium [R] ([AP, 26 Sep 2022](https://www.click2houston.com/news/world/2022/09/26/hardship-remains-for-ukrainian-town-emerging-from-occupation/)):
  - scavenged wood for cooking fires;
  - a lamp made from cotton wick and sunflower oil;
  - one family went several days without eating.
- In Mariupol later in 2022 [R] ([Mediazona, 14 Dec 2022](https://zona.media/article/2022/12/14/mariupolis); [Meduza, 6 Dec 2022](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2022/12/06/we-ve-been-left-to-die)):
  - one family cooked on a 5-litre gas cylinder bought in Taganrog, Russia;
  - others cooked outdoors;
  - one family restored an old stove.

### 2.6 Humanitarian corridors and evacuation

- **Mariupol.**
  - At least seven early attempts at safe passage failed as ceasefires collapsed ([HRW, 21 Mar 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/03/21/ukraine-ensure-safe-passage-aid-mariupol-civilians)). An aid convoy was stuck in Berdiansk and turned back.
  - People finally left in self-organised car convoys from 14 March. One resident estimated at least 50 destroyed cars for every working one [R, single].
  - Ukrainian officials said 4,000 to 4,500 residents were taken into Russia against their will. Russia said nearly 60,000 were "evacuated to Russia" [claims].
- **Azovstal plant.** About 500 people were evacuated with the UN and ICRC on 3–4 May 2022 after weeks underground with dwindling food, water and medicine ([ICRC](https://icrc.org/en/document/more-150-civilians-evacuated-azovstal-and-mariupol-area-third-safe-passage-operation)†). Mariupol's mayor said only 3 of 14 buses reached Ukrainian territory, the rest being "lost" in filtration [claim] ([Meduza, 4 May 2022](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2022/05/04/ukrainian-civilians-evacuated-after-weeks-of-hiding-in-azovstal-steel-plant)).
- **Hostomel.** A basement group reached volunteers on patchy signal and left within hours during a corridor. Shelling hit the column behind them [R, single] ([Al Jazeera, 5 Jul 2022](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2022/7/5/like-ghosts-inside-an-occupied-apartment-building-in-ukraine)).
- **Kherson.**
  - One volunteer group could move at most 300 people a week against 500–600 requests a day [R, single] ([ZMINA, 8 Sep 2022](https://zmina.info/?p=215283)).
  - From late September, men aged 18–35 were reportedly turned back at Vasylivka [R, claim] ([LB.ua, 25 Sep 2022](https://en.lb.ua/news/2022/09/25/17093_russians_ban_men_under_35_leaving.html)†).
  - By late October, evacuation transport out of Kherson and Zaporizhzhia oblasts had effectively stopped [R, claim] ([Ukrainska Pravda, 25 Oct 2022](https://pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2022/10/25/7373422)†).

### 2.7 Collaboration and informers

- The UN found that residents were encouraged to report on one another, leaving people afraid even of friends and neighbours [R] ([OHCHR, 20 Mar 2024](https://ukraine.un.org/en/node/264055)).
- In Melitopol, collaborators reportedly told troops which flats were empty, and residents feared neighbours who might report them [R, single source] ([Kyiv Independent, 22 Apr 2023](https://kyivindependent.com/theyll-kill-me-if-i-come-back-abduction-torture-become-routine-in-russian-occupied-melitopol/)).
- In Enerhodar, detained resistance members were released so that one would inform on another, and families were threatened [R, claim: mayor] ([hromadske, 14 Jul 2022](https://hromadske.ua/en/posts/everyone-should-become-a-partisan-in-their-own-place-how-the-resistance-movement-works)).
- After liberation, collaboration became a social fault line. Collaborators were neighbours, colleagues and relatives, and police hunted those who had not left in time ([search summary of AP, CBC and Novaya Gazeta reporting](https://www.cbc.ca/1.6653982)†).
- The UN also criticised Ukraine's broad collaboration prosecutions in retaken areas ([OHCHR, 20 Mar 2024](https://ukraine.un.org/en/node/264055)).

### 2.8 Neighbours helping each other

- **Bucha:** a man fed others from his own stocks. Neighbours gave first aid and buried the dead in yards. One wounded man was pushed 2.5 km to hospital in a wheelbarrow [R] ([HRW, 21 Apr 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/04/21/ukraine-russian-forces-trail-death-bucha)).
- **Mariupol:** people shared dwindling stocks, and teachers acted as medics in a school shelter. Men melted snow so women and children could wash [R] ([HRW, 21 Mar 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/03/21/ukraine-ensure-safe-passage-aid-mariupol-civilians)).
- **Kherson:**
  - Residents warned each other of search parties by messenger ([hromadske, 27 Nov 2022](https://hromadske.ua/en/posts/eight-months-of-fear-and-resistance-stories-heard-and-seen-in-kherson)).
  - Volunteers distributed food, medicine and nappies and were targeted for it ([HRW, 22 Jul 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/07/22/ukraine-torture-disappearances-occupied-south)).
  - Parcels of medicine were posted in from Ukrainian-held areas ([CIR](https://info-res.org/app/uploads/2024/11/20220920_Kherson-series-1_Mechanisms-of-occupation-FINAL.pdf)).
- **Mariupol, winter:** a mother moved in with a neighbour so they could share heaters [R] ([Mediazona, 14 Dec 2022](https://zona.media/article/2022/12/14/mariupolis)).

## 3. Months

### 3.1 Occupation administration

CIR's Kherson study (September 2022) is the best single account of how administration became the main pressure [R] ([CIR, Sep 2022](https://info-res.org/app/uploads/2024/11/20220920_Kherson-series-1_Mechanisms-of-occupation-FINAL.pdf)):

- Access to money, transport, communications and electricity was progressively tied to Russian passports, bank accounts and registration.
- Each registration (pension, fuel, utilities, SIM card) also built a database of residents.
- Example: firewood and coal for the winter were promised at low prices, but only after residents registered in a database.

### 3.2 Ruble, banks and pensions

**Ruble**

- In April 2022, the occupation administration announced the ruble with a four-month transition alongside the hryvnia ([Al Jazeera, 28 Apr 2022](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/4/28/moscow-to-adopt-ruble-in-ukraine-russian-administrator)†).
- The planned 1 May switch did not happen on time, and cities kept using the hryvnia ([Ukrainska Pravda, 5 May 2022](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2022/05/05/7344281/)†).
- By summer, ambulance workers were pressed to accept ruble pay, according to ISW as reported by Kyiv Independent [claim: Ukrainian intelligence] ([Kyiv Independent summary of ISW](https://kyivindependent.com/institute-for-the-study-of-war-russian-proxies-continue-consolidating-administrative-control-of-occupied-areas-enforce-ruble-payments)†).
- Businesses mostly ignored ruble enforcement in July ([Kyiv Independent, 28 Jul 2022](https://kyivindependent.com/national/amid-brutal-russian-occupation-khersons-resistance-continues-through-boycott-guerilla-operations)).
- Local markets often refused rubles to the end ([hromadske, 27 Nov 2022](https://hromadske.ua/en/posts/eight-months-of-fear-and-resistance-stories-heard-and-seen-in-kherson)).
- In Melitopol by 2023, the ruble had replaced the hryvnia, and holding hryvnias was cited as a reason for detention ([Kyiv Independent, 22 Apr 2023](https://kyivindependent.com/theyll-kill-me-if-i-come-back-abduction-torture-become-routine-in-russian-occupied-melitopol/)).

**Pensions and banks** [R] ([CIR](https://info-res.org/app/uploads/2024/11/20220920_Kherson-series-1_Mechanisms-of-occupation-FINAL.pdf)):

- Ukraine kept paying pensions into hryvnia accounts, but cash could no longer be collected in the occupied area.
- From 30 June, the occupation's "pension fund" paid an initial 10,000 rubles (about £140) in cash to registered pensioners. They had to bring a pension certificate and a passport in person.
- In August, pensioners began moving to cards from Promsvyazbank, a sanctioned Russian state bank. The plan was 5,000 cards, and the administration claimed 937 had been issued by 5 September.
- Businesses had to register and bank with Promsvyazbank, which needed a Russian passport.

### 3.3 Russian passports

**Kherson, 2022** [R] ([CIR](https://info-res.org/app/uploads/2024/11/20220920_Kherson-series-1_Mechanisms-of-occupation-FINAL.pdf)):

- First passports issued 11 June. Over 12,000 applications in the region by 15 August, per Russian sources.
- From 23 August, a "Migration Service" made home visits to take applications from people too frail to come in. CIR notes this also identified who refused.
- Over time, passports were needed for:
  - aid and pension help (one resident said people were asked "where are your Russian passports?");
  - jobs;
  - business registration;
  - a tax number;
  - a utility account;
  - a driving licence and car plates.

**Uptake.** By August 2022, only about 10,000 people in the occupied Kherson and Zaporizhzhia oblasts, roughly 1% of adults, had taken passports, despite 10,000-ruble payments [R, claim] ([Ukrainska Pravda, 1 Aug 2022](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2022/08/01/7361339/)†).

**Who was squeezed.** Refusers faced tighter movement limits, were gradually barred from public-sector jobs, and lost healthcare and social benefits [R] ([OHCHR, 20 Mar 2024](https://ukraine.un.org/en/node/264055)).

**2023 decree.** Putin's decree of 27 April 2023 said Ukrainians in the annexed regions who had not taken Russian citizenship would count as foreign nationals from 1 July 2024 and could be deported [R] ([HRW, 16 May 2023](https://www.hrw.org/news/2023/05/16/russia-threatens-ukrainians-who-refuse-russian-citizenship)†; [Meduza, 28 Apr 2023](https://meduza.io/en/news/2023/04/28/putin-signs-decree-allowing-deportation-of-residents-of-annexed-territories-who-refuse-russian-citizenship)†).

### 3.4 Mobile networks, internet and propaganda

**Kherson SIM cards, 31 May 2022** [R, single] ([Novaya Gazeta Europe, 8 Jun 2022](https://novayagazeta.eu/en/articles/2022/06/08/the-wretched-connection)):

- Cars with loudspeakers had announced that Russia would provide SIM cards.
- Sales started in the former Vodafone office. A crowd gathered by 7 a.m., and tables opened at 10.
- Buyers paid 60 hryvnia per card in cash. Their ID was photographed, and they got no contract or receipt. The limit appeared to be five cards per ID.
- Cards were blank white with no operator name.
- Data barely worked: of ten cards, one had weak internet and nine had none. Coverage was city-only.
- An 82-year-old waited four hours.
- One man in a nearby town walked to a stadium where a Ukrainian hotspot still worked.

**Registration and monitoring.** From late August, SIM users had to give passport details or be cut off. Officials justified it as fighting "terrorist groups," which implies monitoring [R] ([CIR](https://info-res.org/app/uploads/2024/11/20220920_Kherson-series-1_Mechanisms-of-occupation-FINAL.pdf)).

**Control of information.** The UN found Ukrainian internet, mobile, TV and radio shut down and traffic rerouted through Russian networks, giving authorities control of what people could see online [R] ([OHCHR, 20 Mar 2024](https://ukraine.un.org/en/node/264055)).

**Propaganda** [R] ([CIR](https://info-res.org/app/uploads/2024/11/20220920_Kherson-series-1_Mechanisms-of-occupation-FINAL.pdf)):

- Healthcare promises that were not delivered.
- Filmed aid handouts.
- Utility tariff cuts, for example electricity from 3.46 to 2.12 rubles/kWh.
- A first-grade "ABC of Kherson" textbook.

### 3.5 Schools

**Kherson.** Russian curriculum and Russian language from 1 September 2022 [R] ([CIR](https://info-res.org/app/uploads/2024/11/20220920_Kherson-series-1_Mechanisms-of-occupation-FINAL.pdf)).

**Melitopol** [R, claims by the exiled mayor]:

- The head of the city education department was abducted on 28 March 2022 after refusing to reopen schools on a Russian syllabus ([KHPG](https://khpg.org/en/1608810509)†).
- By February 2023, only 10 of 22 schools were open. Fewer than 300 of about 2,900 education staff had agreed to work.
- Troops searched teachers' homes and confiscated devices to stop online Ukrainian lessons ([Kyiv Independent, 6 Feb 2023](https://kyivindependent.com/russian-occupying-forces-raid-teachers-homes-confiscate-electronics-in-melitopol)†).

**Kharkiv region and wider** [R] ([HRW, 20 Jun 2024](https://www.hrw.org/news/2024/06/20/ukraine-forced-russified-education-under-occupation)†):

- Occupying authorities threatened parents whose children studied the Ukrainian curriculum online.
- More than 62,400 children in occupied areas were still studying remotely in Ukrainian schools.
- Russified schools had few classes and staff, and often no electricity.
- The UN also found children enlisted into patriotic youth groups ([OHCHR, 20 Mar 2024](https://ukraine.un.org/en/node/264055)).

### 3.6 The "referendum" and annexation

- UN political chief: besides polling stations, officials went door to door with ballot boxes, accompanied by soldiers [R] ([UN News, 27 Sep 2022](https://news.un.org/en/story/2022/09/1128161)†).
- Kherson, 23–27 September: for the first four days, staff went door to door with armed soldiers or asked passers-by to vote in the street [R] ([openDemocracy](https://ghost.opendemocracy.net/en/russia-ukraine-annexation-kherson-zaporizhzhia-resistance)†).
- Residents said people who declared they would vote for Ukraine were told to leave within a day, and ballot boxes were watched by armed soldiers [R, testimony] ([hromadske, 27 Nov 2022](https://hromadske.ua/en/posts/eight-months-of-fear-and-resistance-stories-heard-and-seen-in-kherson)).
- Annexation was proclaimed on 30 September 2022 ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/9/30/putin-announces-russian-annexation-of-four-ukrainian-regions)†).

### 3.7 Conscription of residents

- **Donetsk and Luhansk (DNR/LNR, occupied before 2022).** Men were seized from streets, workplaces and homes from early 2022 [R, pattern]. Ukrainian military intelligence put the total forcibly mobilised from occupied territory at 55,000–60,000 by July 2023 [claim] ([KHPG, 16 Aug 2023](https://khpg.org/en/1608812640)).
- **Kherson and Zaporizhzhia, autumn 2022.** Men aged 18–35 were reportedly stopped from leaving through Vasylivka from late September [claim] ([LB.ua](https://en.lb.ua/news/2022/09/25/17093_russians_ban_men_under_35_leaving.html)†). On 24 October, the Kherson occupation authorities offered men who stayed the "opportunity" to join local territorial defence units ([Meduza, 24 Oct 2022](https://meduza.io/en/news/2022/10/24/russian-backed-annexation-authorities-in-kherson-announce-formation-of-territorial-defense)†).
- **Melitopol, 2023.** Men aged 20 to 50 were subject to conscription. Refusers were detained repeatedly: 3 days, then a week, then a month [R, single source] ([Kyiv Independent, 22 Apr 2023](https://kyivindependent.com/theyll-kill-me-if-i-come-back-abduction-torture-become-routine-in-russian-occupied-melitopol/)).

### 3.8 Deportation and children

- Filtration fed forcible transfers into Russia and Russian-held areas ([HRW, 1 Sep 2022](https://www.hrw.org/report/2022/09/01/we-had-no-choice/filtration-and-crime-forcibly-transferring-ukrainian-civilians); [Amnesty, 10 Nov 2022](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2022/11/ukraine-russias-unlawful-transfer-of-civilians-a-war-crime-and-likely-a-crime-against-humanity-new-report/)†).
  - Amnesty recorded a mother separated from her 11-year-old son during filtration and never reunited.
  - Amnesty also recorded older people placed in institutions after fleeing.
- Yale HRL found at least 6,000 Ukrainian children held in at least 43 camps and facilities in Crimea and Russia. Most were not orphans, and parents had consented under duress [R] ([Yale HRL, Feb 2023](https://medicine.yale.edu/news-article/russias-systematic-program-for-the-re-education-and-adoption-of-ukraines-children/)†).
- Before Russia left the right bank of the Dnipro, the occupation administration ordered civilians across the river [R]:
  - organised transfers from 18–19 October;
  - Kherson city residents told to leave "immediately" on 22 October ([Meduza, 23 Oct 2022](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2022/10/23/kherson-residents-urged-to-leave-immediately)†);
  - the occupation chief claimed over 70,000 had crossed by 26 October [claim] ([Meduza, 26 Oct 2022](https://meduza.io/en/news/2022/10/26/head-of-annexed-kherson-region-70-000-cross-to-east-bank-of-dnipro)†).

### 3.9 Partisans and reprisals

**Kherson attacks on officials** [R] ([Kyiv Independent, 28 Jul 2022](https://kyivindependent.com/national/amid-brutal-russian-occupation-khersons-resistance-continues-through-boycott-guerilla-operations)):

- An aide to the occupation chief was shot in late March.
- A car bomb wounded the local prison head on 16 June.
- A car bomb killed an occupation official in late June.
- A blast on 27 July killed one of two Russian-installed police staff.

**Everyday civic resistance.** Residents swapped flags on buildings overnight, put up posters, spoke only Ukrainian, and boycotted Russian paperwork ([Kyiv Independent, 28 Jul 2022](https://kyivindependent.com/national/amid-brutal-russian-occupation-khersons-resistance-continues-through-boycott-guerilla-operations)).

**Reprisals and protection** [R, pattern]:

- The Melitopol mayor said every successful resistance action was followed by detentions [claim] ([hromadske, 14 Jul 2022](https://hromadske.ua/en/posts/everyone-should-become-a-partisan-in-their-own-place-how-the-resistance-movement-works)).
- Collaborators began wearing body armour and taking guards.
- After Russian flags were torn down in Melitopol, troops searched with dogs ([Kyiv Independent, 22 Apr 2023](https://kyivindependent.com/theyll-kill-me-if-i-come-back-abduction-torture-become-routine-in-russian-occupied-melitopol/)).
- A teenager in Kherson was imprisoned for ripping down a Russian flag ([KHPG, 25 Nov 2022](https://khpg.org/en/1608811434)).

**Resistance tasks.** Partisans sent troop movements to the army, put up leaflets along patrol routes, and sabotaged rail lines, for example a railway bridge between Melitopol and Tokmak on 3 July 2022 ([hromadske, 14 Jul 2022](https://hromadske.ua/en/posts/everyone-should-become-a-partisan-in-their-own-place-how-the-resistance-movement-works)).

### 3.10 How prices and supply settled

**Kherson pattern (sources in 2.4)** [R]:

- First month: a scarcity spike, prices three to five times higher and medicine gone.
- Months 3–5: cash crisis with a 10–15% commission and a 90% pharmacy collapse.
- From summer: new supply routes from Crimea and Russia, with packaged goods dear and local produce cheap.
- By month 8: food about double pre-war prices, local vegetables at pre-war prices.

**Occupied Mariupol, winter** [R] ([Meduza, 6 Dec 2022](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2022/12/06/we-ve-been-left-to-die); [Mediazona, 14 Dec 2022](https://zona.media/article/2022/12/14/mariupolis)):

- Rubble-clearing paid about 10,000 rubles (about $158) a month.
- A disability benefit was 10,000 rubles, and rent was 3,000–4,000 rubles.
- Food was very expensive. Alcohol cost less than food.
- People from Russia earned high wages on reconstruction while locals got "pennies."

### 3.11 Winter heating, especially 2022–2023

**Occupied Mariupol, December 2022** [R] ([Meduza, 6 Dec 2022](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2022/12/06/we-ve-been-left-to-die)):

- About 100,000 people remained. Up to 90% of apartment buildings were damaged or destroyed.
- One flat fell to 3 °C after wind tore the plastic film on its windows.
- Space heaters overloaded the grid when many people switched them on together.
- Many people lived on staircases and in basements because their flats had become wind tunnels.
- No firewood or coal was handed out, and many could not afford it.
- Bread truck queue: it arrived at 11:00, about 10 km from one family's home. People queued from 5 a.m. Someone arriving at 6:00 was 600th in line, and only 300 loaves came, so fewer than one loaf for every two people queuing [D].
- Repairs promised by 10 October had not happened in December.

**Occupied Mariupol, mid-December** [R] ([Mediazona, 14 Dec 2022](https://zona.media/article/2022/12/14/mariupolis)):

- Residents reported no heat at all, or heat only from space heaters.
- One mother said that with heaters placed together and everyone sleeping close, you could live. She moved in with a neighbour.
- Everyone was coughing. One woman had acute bronchitis, and sick leave was unpaid.
- Some people still cooked outdoors.

**Luhansk oblast (Sievierodonetsk, Rubizhne), winter 2022–23**

- People cut almost all the city trees for firewood [R, claim: Ukrainian regional head] ([Ukrainska Pravda, 26 Dec 2022](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2022/12/26/7382404)†).
- KHPG reported a second occupation winter in Sievierodonetsk without heating and with blown-out windows still unreplaced ([KHPG](https://khpg.org/en/1608813075)†).

**WHO warning for Ukraine as a whole, winter 2022–23** [R] ([WHO Europe, 21 Nov 2022](https://www.who.int/europe/news-room/21-11-2022-statement---winter-in-ukraine--people-s-health-cannot-be-held-hostage)):

- Temperatures expected down to −20 °C in places, with 10 million people without power.
- People would turn to charcoal, wood, diesel generators and electric heaters, with risks of toxic exposure and burns.
- Respiratory infections, pneumonia and influenza expected.

### 3.12 Kherson, months 6 to 9, and the end

**Months 6–7 (September 2022)**

- Passports, ruble pensions, Russian bank cards and plates spread, and schools switched curriculum ([CIR](https://info-res.org/app/uploads/2024/11/20220920_Kherson-series-1_Mechanisms-of-occupation-FINAL.pdf)).
- Kherson had about 290,000 people before the war, and up to 180,000 remained in May [R] ([Kyiv Independent, 28 Jul 2022](https://kyivindependent.com/national/amid-brutal-russian-occupation-khersons-resistance-continues-through-boycott-guerilla-operations)). Ukraine's deputy prime minister urged an estimated 450,000–500,000 remaining residents of the region to leave.

**Months 7–8**

- Referendum and annexation, then exit bans for young men and offers to join local units (3.6, 3.7).
- From 19 October, the "evacuation" across the river (3.8).

**Final week**

- On 4 November, a round-the-clock curfew was announced, then withdrawn (1.4).
- On 6 November, the city lost power and water. The occupation blamed sabotage, and Ukraine blamed Russian attacks ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/11/6/russian-occupied-kherson-loses-power-after-alleged-sabotage)†; [Meduza](https://meduza.io/en/news/2022/11/06/lights-go-out-in-kherson-russia-calls-it-terrorist-attack-on-a-transmission-line)†).
- Residents lost Russian mobile service in the weeks before the retreat ([Meduza, 14 Nov 2022](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2022/11/14/the-state-of-liberated-kherson)).
- The TV tower was destroyed on the eve of withdrawal ([hromadske, 27 Nov 2022](https://hromadske.ua/en/posts/eight-months-of-fear-and-resistance-stories-heard-and-seen-in-kherson)).

**Liberation, 11 November** [R]

- The power company said the entire energy system had been destroyed, with up to a month to restore it ([Meduza, 14 Nov 2022](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2022/11/14/the-state-of-liberated-kherson)).
- There was no water, electricity or heating. The railway station was the only place with steady power, and residents charged phones from generators ([hromadske, 27 Nov 2022](https://hromadske.ua/en/posts/eight-months-of-fear-and-resistance-stories-heard-and-seen-in-kherson)).
- Ukraine imposed a 17:00–08:00 curfew. Demining was expected to take months, and police opened 400 war-crime cases on the first day ([Meduza, 14 Nov 2022](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2022/11/14/the-state-of-liberated-kherson)).
- Animals were taken from the city zoo.
- One resident said that with checkpoints everywhere you felt you could be shot at any time, and that the fear lifted at liberation ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/1.6653982)†).
- In Balakliia, retreating troops left people locked in the police-station cells, and they broke out through a window ([Meduza, 16 Sep 2022](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2022/09/16/anyone-could-be-taken-captive-at-any-time)).

### 3.13 Rural areas

- **Yahidne** (village basement, 28 days) and **Nova Basan** (all men on a street detained): see 1.1 and 1.7 ([HRW, 18 May 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/05/18/ukraine-executions-torture-during-russian-occupation)).
- **Balakliia area.** Older women kept working gardens under fire and called themselves "kamikaze women." Soldiers took food and killed farm animals. A 67-year-old villager was hooded and taken because soldiers accused his son-in-law of helping Ukraine's army [R] ([Meduza, 16 Sep 2022](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2022/09/16/anyone-could-be-taken-captive-at-any-time)).
- **Kherson region villages** [R] ([BBC via RNZ, 24 Oct 2022](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/world/477279/ukraine-war-the-cost-of-occupation-in-kherson-region)):
  - a villager said they almost starved in the first months, and his car, furniture and mattresses were taken;
  - after liberation, people relied on volunteers for food, with no power or water;
  - fields were dotted with unexploded missiles.
- **Farms.** Farmers were reportedly told they could sow only if they handed over 70% of the harvest, and grain and machinery were taken [claim: Ukrainian officials] ([Euromaidan Press, 29 Apr 2022](https://euromaidanpress.com/2022/04/29/threatening-kherson-farmers-russian-troops-steal-grain-from-ukraine/)†). The second OSCE Moscow Mechanism mission found signs of organised export of Ukrainian grain ([Ukraine's statement summarising the OSCE report, 14 Jul 2022](https://www.osce.org/files/f/documents/4/6/524727.pdf); [OSCE](https://www.osce.org/odihr/522691)†).
- **Izium.** A city councillor claimed over 1,000 civilians died under occupation [claim] ([Ukrainska Pravda, 12 Sep 2022](https://pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2022/09/12/7367140)†). Ukrainian investigators exhumed 447 bodies from one burial site, most of them civilians ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/news-feed/state-emergency-service-447-bodies-found-at-mass-burial-site-in-liberated-izium)†).

## 5. Gaps and cautions

- No source counts searches per flat. Search frequency in the game is [P], shaped by the sweep-then-targeted pattern in 1.2.
- Torture is described here only as far as a design decision needs: frequency, length and outcome. Method detail was left out on purpose.
- Several Melitopol, Kherson and Vasylivka figures come from the exiled Melitopol mayor or Ukrainian prosecutors, and are labelled claims.
- The cold data stops around −5 °C to +5 °C indoors and −20 °C outdoors (WHO forecast). Heihe's January needs extrapolation, labelled [D]/[P].
- ISW, Ukrainska Pravda and IWPR could not be opened directly. Their points are marked † and are worth rechecking by hand before they become rules.

## 6. What this suggests for the game

Each mechanic lists its tag and the sections it rests on. Adapting things to Heihe (WeChat in place of Telegram, yuan in place of hryvnia, Chinese ID cards) is a [P] translation, and the shape is unchanged.

1. **Two kinds of district.** Each district is either "assault" or "administered" when the occupation starts.
   - In assault districts, power, water and mobile fail within a day, gas within about a week, and soldiers kill on contact during the first weeks.
   - In administered districts, utilities keep running for months, and pressure comes from paperwork and security services.
   - [R] shape and timings (0, 1.8); which Heihe districts get which is [P].

2. **Day-2 order sheet.** The commandant posts rules:
   - curfew 20:00–06:00;
   - walk alone or in pairs;
   - cars at walking speed, accepting searches;
   - stop at the first shout.
   Breaking a rule raises the chance you are shot or detained. [R] rule set (1.4, Kherson); penalty sizes [P].

3. **Moving is the danger, especially for men.** In the first weeks, each trip outside rolls for being shot, scaled up for men, cars and darkness. Fetching water, carrying food to others and visiting relatives are exactly the trips that got people killed. The risk falls sharply once the administration settles in. [R] shape (1.5: 43 people killed while moving, 77% of victims men); per-trip odds [P].

4. **Sweep, then targeted visits.**
   - In the first 72 hours, soldiers sweep every building in a district. Residents are ordered to the basement with doors unlocked, flats are searched for weapons, radios and phones, and some men are taken.
   - After that, visits come in waves through a neighbourhood and target listed people.
   - A neighbour network gives warning (a message that "they are on our street") if the player has built trust with neighbours.
   - [R] shape (1.1, 1.2); wave timing [P].

5. **The phone is evidence.**
   - The player's phone holds items with risk values: channels or groups, photos of military vehicles, messages about troop movements, banking or state apps, chats with relatives in the army.
   - A check takes about 20 minutes and can copy contacts.
   - An empty phone with no Russian social-media account is mildly suspicious too.
   - A cheap "clean phone" is a valid strategy, and so is leaving the phone at home (which gives up warnings).
   - [R] (1.3); risk weights [P].

6. **Body checks for men.** At filtration and some checkpoints, men are checked for military or nationalist tattoos, rifle-recoil bruises on the shoulder, and gun oil on the fingers. Recent manual work and old tattoos carry risk. [R] (1.3); about 1 in 15 road checkpoints does a strip check [D from a single account]; filtration always does.

7. **The watchlist.** The security service builds a list from seized city records, then widens it:
   - Tier 1: officials, police, Territorial Defence, veterans of 2014–22, journalists, organisers of protests.
   - Tier 2: volunteers, teachers, priests, relatives of soldiers.
   - Tier 3, from about month 2: anyone reported as opposed.
   Getting onto a tier comes from the player's background and from actions such as handing out aid. [R] (2.2); tier timing [P].

8. **Detention as a survivable event.**
   - Being detained takes the player off the map for a while. Lengths: about 40% under 4 days, 35% 4–30 days, 20% 1–4 months, 5% moved to Crimea or Russia or never seen again.
   - The player loses phone, cash and car, and loses weight (one case: 20 kg in 16 days).
   - Release can come with strings: sign to "cooperate", film a statement, become an informant, or be barred from leaving town.
   - A small share die in custody, about 2% per detention.
   - [R] shapes (2.3); the split is [P] fitted to the reported range; death rate [D] from Yale.

9. **Background detention rate.** Each NPC faces about 0.1% a month of being taken, weighted about 7:1 toward men, rising after partisan attacks. With a few hundred NPCs in a district, a few disappear each season. [D] (4).

10. **Informers and empty flats.** Some neighbours report. Flats left empty get reported and looted first. Leaving a flat empty for long, or having a reputation for helping, raises the odds of a visit. [R] (2.7, 1.6); probabilities [P].

11. **Checkpoint geography.**
    - Inside the city, 2–3 fixed checkpoints on bridges and main roads, with waits of minutes to hours. Each pass checks documents, sometimes the phone, sometimes the body.
    - Leaving takes about 30 checkpoints plus a crossing queue of 4–10 days, living in the car, at 3,000–7,000 UAH-equivalent for a driver.
    - The crossing closes to men aged 18–35 from about month 7.
    - [R] (2.1, 3.7); costs in yuan [P].

12. **Filtration slip as an internal pass.** After filtration (hours to weeks), the player gets a paper slip. Without it, work and movement within the city get harder. Losing it is a real loss. [R] (2.1, Mariupol).

13. **Siege water ladder.** Sources, from best to worst:
    - water truck or spring: 1–3 km away, up to 6 h queue, exposed to shelling and to fire;
    - well or ground water, which must be boiled;
    - rain or snow, which costs fuel;
    - water drained from radiators or the heating system, which is limited and dirty.
    Need is 15 L a day for full health and 3 L a day to survive. Each 6 L bottle is weight to carry. [R] (2.5, 4); Sphere numbers [R].

14. **Cold costs food and health.**
    - Calorie need = 2,100 + 100 for every 5 °C of indoor mean below 20 °C.
    - Below 18 °C indoors, the risk of respiratory illness builds day by day. Elderly, children and the chronically ill are worse off.
    - Sleeping together near one heater and moving into a staircase or basement are valid answers.
    - If everyone runs heaters at once, the grid trips.
    - [R] base rule and threshold (4, 3.11); illness rates [P].

15. **Heating fuel runs out in the city.** The fuel order of use is wood scraps, furniture, then city trees. The administration offers firewood or coal only after you register in its database. Improvised heating carries burn and fume risks. [R] (3.1, 3.11); fuel quantities [P].

16. **Cash crisis and the cash-out trade.**
    - Banks close, and ATMs work for a few weeks with a dawn queue and a daily cap.
    - After that, cash only comes from private sellers at a 10–15% cut.
    - Bartering and selling belongings at a flea market are fallbacks.
    - The occupier's money is refused at markets at first, then becomes compulsory. By year 2, holding the old money is suspicious.
    - [R] (2.4, 3.2).

17. **Price curve with two tracks.**
    - Packaged and imported goods: 3–5× in month 1, settling to about 2× by month 6.
    - Medicine: 3–5× by month 3, and most pharmacies closed.
    - Local produce stays near the pre-war price.
    - Official aid parcels are small, about one parcel per person every three weeks: a few tins, a kilogram of groats, half a loaf a week.
    - [R] (2.4, 3.10).

18. **Medicine clock.** NPCs and the player can depend on a daily drug (insulin, heart medicine, thyroid medicine) or on electricity (an oxygen concentrator). Without supply, harm begins at about day 7 and deaths at about day 10–14. The electricity-dependent die when the power goes. [D] from siege timeline (2.4, 4).

19. **The passport ladder.** Over months, more doors need the occupier's passport:
    - month 3–4: pension and aid;
    - month 5: SIM registration, utility account, bank card;
    - month 6: car plates and driving licence, business registration, public-sector jobs;
    - year 2: healthcare and legal residence.
    A "Migration Service" home visit records who refuses. Taking the passport opens doors and closes the way back, since collaboration is prosecuted later. [R] order (3.1, 3.3, 2.7); exact months [P] fitted to the Kherson timeline.

20. **Communications.**
    - The old network is cut at about month 3.
    - New SIM cards are sold for cash in a queue, with your ID photographed, five per ID, and data barely working. Passport registration is required by month 6.
    - Specific spots (a stadium, a bank lobby) still pick up the old network's Wi-Fi or signal.
    - A VPN works but raises suspicion.
    - [R] (3.4); [P] where the spots are.

21. **School as a pressure point.** Russian curriculum from the start of the next school year. Teaching or studying the old curriculum online is a secret act that brings device confiscation and threats against parents. Children can be sent on "rest camp" trips that may not return. [R] (3.5, 3.8).

22. **Referendum day.** For about four days, officials with armed soldiers go door to door with a ballot box. Refusing marks the household. Voting "no" brings an order to leave within a day. [R] (3.6).

23. **Men and conscription.** After annexation, men 18–35 cannot leave. Offers to join a local unit follow, and in year 2 call-up papers arrive. Refusing means repeated detentions of growing length (3 days, a week, a month). Hiding is possible but cuts the player off from aid and paperwork. [R] (3.7); hiding rules [P].

24. **Partisans and reprisals.** Resistance acts (flag swaps, leaflets, passing on troop positions, sabotage) have real effect, and each successful one triggers a reprisal wave: more checkpoint phone checks, dog searches of the area, and a spike in detentions. [R] (3.9); wave size [P].

25. **Endgame shapes.** If the front moves, the occupier:
    - orders civilians away, by "evacuation" across the river;
    - cuts power, water and the TV mast;
    - takes what it can, even zoo animals;
    - may leave detainees locked in the cells.
    After it leaves, the player's record (passport, job, informer role) is judged by the returning authorities. [R] (3.12, 2.7).
