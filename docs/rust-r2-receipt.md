# R2 receipt: the rest of Heihe's buildings

16 November 2026. Branch `claude/rust-heihe`. The owner said 下載並補齊: download CMAB and fill in the town.

## The data

- `heilongjiang.zip` from figshare 27992417 (CMAB v7, CC BY 4.0): 414,319,581 bytes, MD5 `1f21f2de9813547fef849bb26c13176b`, the same as the publisher lists. It is kept in `data/raw/cmab-v7/` and not committed.
- The province comes as 200 layers numbered by city. The one whose bounds contain central Heihe was found by reading each shapefile's header: `processed_1809_20230514_all` (imagery of 2023-05-14, Web Mercator). Only that layer was extracted. SHA-256 of its `.shp`: `26f13ec5aaf1953405d6f5f917109856c04628aa2241e6d2ade562c7e497a4b0`; `.dbf`: `ec1ffc5e55d32ee100f3cea6a663c53d5bbfcfb1f41216a7eadd78cad9bb67a4`.
- `tools/cmab/slice_heihe.py` cuts 127.4780–127.5110 E, 50.2420–50.2580 N and reprojects to the game's metre grid (the same origin and formula as the OpenStreetMap importer). It writes `data/heihe/cmab.json`: 853 records holding 877 outer rings. CMAB's attributes there: Residence 554, Business 136, Public service 130, Other 29, Office 4; heights from 12.2 m (p10) through 20.7 m (median) to 51.2 m (max). 784 records give the year 1986, which looks like a default rather than a date.

## How the rules were decided

Four checkers looked at the merge from different sides (position and duplicates, heights, the river edge and empty ground, the merge code), each rebuilt the merge independently and reproduced the Rust result exactly before measuring anything, and each proposal then went to a separate skeptic told to refute it. Their scripts, pictures and numbers are in `out/check/` (not committed). What survived, and is now in `crates/sim/src/cmab.rs`:

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Move CMAB 2 m west and 6 m north before anything else | Its roofs sit that far from OpenStreetMap's buildings and streets in this layer. Measured by matched pairs, by best overlap, by least squares on long walls, and independently against the streets; the east-west part came out between 1.5 and 3 m, and 2 m does as well as either. |
| Every outer ring is a roof; holes are left filled | 23 records hold 24 more separate buildings (one holds two); the first version threw them away (22 kept now, from 21 records, 12,429 m²). The 10 holes are light wells and yards of at most 9 m across. |
| Simplify outlines at 0.3 m | CMAB traces roofs as chains of centimetre steps; walls are drawn per edge and edges under 0.3 m got none, leaving see-through slits in 409 buildings. The area of every kept roof changes by at most 1.7%. If simplifying would make an outline cross itself, the tolerance is halved, the last try at exactly 1 cm; if even that does not help, the roof is dropped as a bad shape. No roof in this slice comes near that. |
| Drop roofs under 20 m² or narrower than 2.8 m | All 26 under 20 m² were specks and slivers; the widest artefact is 2.59 m, the narrowest real roof (a row of sheds) 3.05 m. |
| Drop roofs on the river (centre within 3 m of the bank), on the promenade the game paves, over a landmark or the Mother Square | One 6,697 m² "roof" covered the Mother sculpture and its whole square; the 2017 photograph shows open ground. |
| Drop roofs a street or footpath runs 10 m or more through, or with more than a quarter of the roof on a carriageway | One cut 官渡路 in two. |
| OpenStreetMap wins both ways: drop a roof 30% under OSM buildings, or one that takes in 30% of an OSM building | The second test caught an L-shaped copy of two OSM buildings. |
| Heights: see `height_rule` | CMAB's median, 20.7 m, is the six-to-seven-storey block of the photographs. Small roofs get ordinary heights from CMAB, so below 120 m² area decides. 13–33 m becomes 5–7 storeys. 33–47 m becomes 7–10: that is a choice to keep the town low, not a reading. In Shanghai's measured pairs this band's median is 8 storeys, but only a third are 7–10 and four in ten are 11 or more; the cap lowered the mean error against those pairs. From 47 m a tower. Roofs of 120–150 m² that CMAB reads at 13 m or more stay at 5–7 storeys. Big shops and public buildings get 4 m storeys. A proposal to cap Central Street at 7 storeys was refuted: a photograph shows an 8-storey building there. |
| OpenStreetMap's guessed heights | On this bank they were never measured: the importer guessed from footprint area and drew towers by a hash of the id. Where one CMAB roof covers at least half of an OSM building and is at most twice its size, that roof's reading is used, with the smaller of the two areas; the hashed towers no CMAB roof confirms come down to 7 storeys. The data check had measured a blend of every roof over a building instead; the final review found the blend let a roof covering 47% and another covering 3% turn a 126 m² two-storey building into seven storeys, so it is one roof now. (Counts are under "Result" below.) |
| Every CMAB building is solid | Judging by its centre left five you could walk into. |

Result: 877 roofs read; 669 kept, 138 under OpenStreetMap, 4 on a street, 29 on the river, 31 too small or a sliver, 6 on the riverfront. With OpenStreetMap's 198, the Chinese bank has 867 buildings. Of OpenStreetMap's 197 guessed heights on this bank, one CMAB roof matches 85 (71 of them change storeys, 2 more only get 4 m storeys) and 4 hashed towers no roof confirms come down to 7 storeys: 77 changed in all. Storeys: 1: 43, 2: 73, 3: 34, 4: 56, 5: 203, 6: 145, 7: 269, 8: 30, 9: 10, 10: 1, 17: 3 (the three towers are outside the playable area). Kinds: tan tile 277, yellow with crimson bands 277, salmon with yellow bays 158, low brick 141, Central Street 14. The tally and the OSM-height counts are pinned in a test.

Not done, and why: 23 roofs west of x = −987 m are dropped as "on the river" because the bank line in `city.json` stops at x = −821 m; they are 244 m beyond the playable edge and on screen only zoomed far out. The fix is to export the whole river edge from the OpenStreetMap importer. Wings of CMAB roofs that stick out from under OpenStreetMap buildings (about 13,000 m²) are lost with the roof; recovering them needs polygon clipping and a person looking at each.

## Things the fill made necessary

- **Cut-away.** With the blocks filled in, a building stands between the camera and you most of the time. Each building on the Chinese bank is its own mesh. One that is really in the way (a line from the camera to your feet, chest, head or shoulders passes into it, parapet included, or through the drawn triangles of the corner crown on a Central Street building, before reaching you) turns to glass and loses its windows. A screen-space box is only the first, cheap pass: on its own it was wrong about four times in ten, and it cut away the hotel you stand in front of on 邮政路 (now a test). A two-centimetre speck made the same way as the buildings carries the glass material from the start, so the shader a cut-away needs is built before the first one. (The first version of the speck was a plain cube without vertex colours, which builds a different shader; the final review caught it.)
- **Camera distance.** Zoomed all the way out, the ground nearest the camera fell behind it and was clipped. The camera now stands about 525 m back; it is orthographic, so nothing on screen changes size. The shadows are one slab that follows the zoom and holds just what is on screen, from the nearest roof to the farthest ground: about 0.09 m a shadow texel at zoom 1, as in R1. The three fixed cascades this round first had were 0.15–0.20 m at zoom 1, and the third of them drew nothing on screen at any zoom closer than about 0.34.
- **Pictures.** `--shots` now draws to the hidden window and captures that. Drawing to an image target, as R1 did, grows memory by about 80 MB a second with 2,600 meshes in Bevy 0.20 until the GPU gives up (it did, twice, at the fourth view); drawing to the window holds steady at 1.36 GB. It waits ten seconds for the meshes to upload, waits for each picture to reach the disk before moving on, and stands the player where a person could stand.
- **Frame times**, written to `frame_times.txt` by `--shots`, at 3200 × 1800 (this laptop's window at 200% scaling), debug build with optimised dependencies, Intel Arc 140V, without waiting for the screen's refresh. Medians of three runs on the committed build: 12 ms at the Mother Square, 21–36 ms elsewhere, 29 ms for the whole town. These numbers are not steady. Every run was started with no other session's test browser running, yet the third ran most views about twice as fast as the first two, and stalled at two (176 and 241 ms, near Bevy's 250 ms cap on a frame's step); this laptop is shared with other work and nothing more was found out. Three earlier runs, on the build before the last crown change, gave 13 ms, 23–41 ms and 38 ms. All six are in `docs/shots/rust-r2/frame_times.txt`. The first measurements this round waited for the screen's refresh, so nothing could read below 16.7 ms. It is not yet fast enough; see below.

## Final review

Before committing, four reviewers each read one side of the round (the claims in the documents against the code and data, the game crate, the sim crate, and how much the tests would catch), and each finding went to a separate agent told to refute it. Their work is in `out/review/` (not committed). What stood, and what was done:

- **Claims.** Eight wrong or loose statements in the receipt, CREDITS and code comments (counts of multi-part records, the 33–47 m band described as a reading when it is a choice, the 120–150 m² clamp left out, the OSM-height counts, an unversioned DOI, frame times credited to R1, which never measured any). All corrected above.
- **Game.** The speck built the wrong shader (fixed). The shadow cascades did not fit the new camera distance (now one slab that follows the zoom). The frame times were capped by the screen's refresh (now measured without it, below). The shots module still described R1's way of taking pictures (fixed). A fifth finding, that the camera offset was written out in four places, was refuted, since all four gave the same direction; it is one constant now anyway.
- **Sim.** The OSM-height correction blended several roofs while everything said one roof (it is one roof now; 77 changed instead of 84). The cut-away judged the roof line while the walls are drawn 0.7 m higher (the parapet counts now: about 2% of real occlusions were missed), and left out the corner crowns (they are judged now; see the second check below). Simplifying could in principle make an outline cross itself (now refused; none does in this slice).
- **Tests.** The 25 tests let 35 of 55 deliberate mistakes through, among them leaving CMAB out of the game altogether, dropping the 2 m west, 6 m north shift, removing the riverfront rule and raising the OSM-overlap threshold to 90%. The reviewer's 18 new tests are in.

A second check of the same kind went over the changes the review led to. The shadow slab held up: at every zoom from 0.12 to 3 and every hour of daylight, every surface on screen lies inside it, except a band a few pixels wide at one edge in the half hour before sunset, which comes from how Bevy fits any cascade to the view (the old three did the same). What stood:

- **Crowns.** The first fix judged each crown as a 4 m column from the ground to the tip, and 81% of the cuts it added were on air: beside the spire, outside the walls, or from inside the wall. The cut-away now tests the six sight lines against the crown's own drawn triangles, and the game builds each building's occluder with the same sim function the tests call. The drums were square to the map, so on buildings that are not, they stood up to 0.6 m out of the facade; they are now turned with the wall and moved in until all four corners are inside. (A third finding, that the gables were left out, was refuted: every sight line that crosses a gable unseen crosses it from behind, where it is not drawn, and a line can reach the front of a gable only through the wall below it, which the cut-away already catches.)
- **Claims.** 73 changed storeys should have been 71 (two more only get taller storeys); the 120–150 m² clamp only applies from a 13 m reading; the simplifier's last try was 1.9 cm, not 1 cm (it is 1 cm now); the 1.7% area figure holds for kept roofs only. All corrected above.
- **Tests.** The river margin past the end of the bank line, the promenade's exact width, and the one-roof rule were each guarded only loosely, by the pinned tally or with slack; each has its own made-up-town test now. A test fixture depended on an exact floating-point tie and was moved off it.

That makes 48 tests.

## Checks

- `cargo test -p sim`: 48 tests, all passing. The fill counts every outer ring in the file; kept roofs are solid, at least 2.8 m wide, never take in 30% of an OSM building, never stand on the river, the promenade, a landmark or Central Street, and no street runs 10 m through one; no OSM hashed tower is left and nothing in the playable area is over 10 storeys; the median is 5–7 storeys; every wall gets drawn; every corner of a crown's drum stands inside its building, the crown the cut-away judges is the one drawn, and both kinds of crown reach their tips; the game gets one block per building, CMAB included. Each merge rule is also tested on a small made-up town (the shift, slivers and specks, the promenade and Mother Square, the river past the end of the bank line, streets through roofs, OpenStreetMap winning both ways, the OSM-height correction with one roof and the smaller area, holes and second buildings in one record); over at least 30 matched pairs, the median offset between CMAB and OpenStreetMap is under 1.5 m on each axis; simplifying changes no kept roof by more than 2%; and the tally above is pinned. The line-in-polygon measure, the simplifier (including the self-crossing case) and the height rule have unit tests; the cut-away leaves the building behind you alone and takes the one in front, a tall one further off, a big one whose sight lines end inside it, a narrow one over your shoulder and a crown above a roof, but not a shed you can be seen over.
- `northeast --shots out/shots`: all eleven views written; I looked at the Central Street, housing, whole-town, Mother Square, shop-board and night views. Four are in `docs/shots/rust-r2/`, scaled to 1600 × 900.

## Not checked, not done

- Nobody has walked the filled town by hand.
- 21–36 ms a frame at full resolution is too slow, and the measurements are too unsteady to judge small changes by; a quieter machine or many more runs are needed. The likely costs are 4× MSAA at 3200 × 1800 and 2,600 separate meshes; neither has been measured on its own. One lead: at the same spot and zoom, noon is slower than dusk in all six runs (13–37 ms against 12–28 ms on Central Street).
- The 23 western roofs and the lost wings above.
- Everything R1 listed as not drawn yet (the goods-street arch, the Ferris wheel, wires, three-wheelers, people) is still not drawn.
